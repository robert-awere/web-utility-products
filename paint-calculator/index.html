How to calculate the amount of paint

We calculate the four walls, subtract approximately 1.9 m² per door and 1.4 m² per window, add the ceiling if selected, then account for your coats and waste allowance.

Paint = (paintable area × coats ÷ coverage) × (1 + waste %)

Coverage varies by paint, surface and application method. Check the figure printed on your paint tin and replace the default when available.

Common paint calculator questions

How much paint do I need for a 10x10 wall?

A 10 × 10 ft wall is 100 square feet. At 400 ft² per gallon, two coats plus 10% waste require about 0.55 gallon. One gallon is therefore a practical purchase and leaves paint for touch-ups.

How do I calculate the amount of paint?

Calculate the paintable wall area, multiply by the number of coats, divide by the coverage rate shown on the paint tin, then add your waste allowance.

Why add 10% extra?

It covers small measuring differences, surface texture and paint left in trays or rollers. Reduce it for a very simple room or increase it for rough surfaces.

Does primer count as a coat?

No. Calculate primer separately because its coverage can differ from the finish paint.

Should I subtract every opening?

Usually, subtracting standard doors and windows is enough. For unusually large openings, reduce the room dimensions or adjust the result manually.

Is my information stored?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser and sends no measurements anywhere.

Paint calculator by square feet or metres

Measure the room’s length, width and wall height, then choose the number of coats. The calculator finds the area of all four walls, subtracts standard doors and windows, and divides that area by the paint’s coverage rate. It can also include the ceiling and a waste allowance.

The large result is the practical amount to plan for. The exact unrounded estimate appears underneath it, so you can compare the answer with the tin sizes available in your shop.

Paint cost calculator: estimate your budget

To estimate paint cost, multiply the practical amount shown above by the shop price per litre or gallon. For example, if the calculator recommends 7 litres and paint costs £18 per litre, the estimated paint cost is £126. Add primer and supplies separately.

Worked paint example

A 4 m × 3 m room with 2.4 m walls, two coats, one door, one window, 10 m²/L coverage and 10% waste needs about 6.67 L. Planning for 7 L gives a small buffer.

How to improve your paint estimate

  1. Measure the longest points of each wall rather than rounding down.
  2. Use the coverage rate printed on your chosen paint tin; porous or textured surfaces may need more paint.
  3. Calculate primer separately because primer and finish paint can have different coverage.
  4. For several rooms, calculate each room separately and add only paints with the same colour and finish.

This calculator is a planning aid, not a guarantee. Surface condition, application method and paint formulation affect real coverage.
