    Upload vs download speed

    Download speed is how fast data reaches you: streaming, web pages, game updates. Upload speed is how fast you send data out: video calls, cloud backups, sending large files, live streaming. Cable and DSL upload is often a fraction of download; fiber is often symmetric. If calls freeze while streaming is fine, upload is usually the bottleneck.

    What is ping (latency) and jitter?

    Ping, or latency, is the round-trip time for a tiny message to reach a server and come back, measured in milliseconds. Lower is better: under 20 ms feels instant, under 50 ms is fine for most games, and over 100 ms starts to feel sluggish in calls and fast-paced games. Jitter is how much that delay varies from one moment to the next. High jitter causes choppy audio and in-game lag even when average ping looks good. SpeedFlex reports the median of about 20 idle pings, and jitter as the average change between consecutive pings.

    What is bufferbloat (loaded latency)?

    Bufferbloat is the lag that appears when your connection is busy. When a big download or upload starts, routers can queue too much data and every other packet waits in line, so a call stutters the moment someone else starts a download. SpeedFlex keeps pinging during the download and upload phases and grades the increase:

    • A+: less than 5 ms increase
    • A: less than 30 ms
    • B: less than 60 ms
    • C: less than 200 ms
    • D: less than 400 ms
    • F: 400 ms or more

    A poor grade is often fixable: turning on Smart Queue Management (SQM) or QoS in your router, if it supports it, can bring loaded latency close to idle latency at the cost of a little peak speed.

    Why is my internet slower than what I pay for?

    Plan speeds are usually "up to" figures measured at the modem with a cable. Common reasons for a lower result, and what to try:

    • Wi-Fi: walls, distance and neighbouring networks cut speed sharply. Move closer to the router, use the 5 GHz or 6 GHz band, or test with an Ethernet cable to see your true line speed.
    • Your device: older phones, laptops and Wi-Fi adapters may top out well below a fast plan. Try another device.
    • Time of day: evenings are peak time on shared networks. Test at a few different times before drawing conclusions.
    • VPN: a VPN adds a detour and encryption overhead. Disconnect it and test again.
    • Other activity: background updates, cloud sync, other people streaming, and busy browser tabs all compete for the same connection. Pause them while testing.
    • Server distance: results depend on the route to the test server. A distant or congested route lowers speed and raises ping.

    If wired tests stay well below your plan, restart the modem and router, then contact your provider.

    How SpeedFlex measures your speed

    SpeedFlex has no test server of its own. Your browser connects to Cloudflare's global edge network, usually to a nearby data centre. It measures idle latency first, then downloads using up to six parallel streams with growing request sizes, then uploads the same way, pinging throughout to measure loaded latency. Throughput is sampled in short rolling windows, and the reported speed is the 90th percentile of the stable windows, so a slow start or a single hiccup does not drag the figure down. The full test usually takes about 12–15 seconds. Data use scales with your speed: roughly 50–100 MB on a 100 Mbps connection and up to around 700 MB on a gigabit line, so take care on metered or mobile data.

    Worked example

    Say a test shows 240 Mbps download, 22 Mbps upload, 18 ms ping, and ping rising by 12 ms under load.

    • Bufferbloat grade A: a 12 ms increase is under the 30 ms threshold.
    • 9 simultaneous 4K streams: 240 ÷ 25 = 9.6, rounded down to 9.
    • 5 HD video calls: calls are limited by upload, and 22 ÷ 3.8 = 5.8, rounded down to 5.
    • Gaming ready: 18 ms idle and 30 ms under load are both comfortable.
    • A 50 GB game in about 28 minutes: 50 GB is 400,000 megabits, and 400,000 ÷ 240 ≈ 1,667 seconds.

    Assumptions and limitations

    SpeedFlex gives a practical estimate, not a certified measurement. Results vary with your device, browser, Wi-Fi conditions and the network route to the Cloudflare server you reach, and a browser test may read lower than a wired test run on the router itself. Activity figures above are typical values, not guarantees.

    Speed test FAQ

    Is this internet speed test free?

    Yes. SpeedFlex is free, needs no account or app, and shows no ads before or during your result.

    How much speed do I need for 4K streaming?

    About 25 Mbps per 4K stream. Add 25 Mbps for each screen that might stream 4K at the same time, plus headroom for everything else on the connection.

    What is a good ping for gaming?

    Under 20 ms is excellent, under 50 ms is good for most online games, and over 100 ms is noticeable. Low jitter and a good bufferbloat grade matter as much as the idle ping.

    Why is my Wi-Fi speed test lower than my plan?

    Wi-Fi signal, distance from the router, older devices, VPNs and other activity on the network all reduce speed. Test with an Ethernet cable close to the router to see what your line itself delivers.

    How much data does a speed test use?

    It depends on your speed, because the test downloads and uploads for a fixed time. Expect roughly 50 to 100 MB on a 100 Mbps connection and up to about 700 MB on a gigabit connection.

    Does SpeedFlex store my results?

    Not on any server of ours. Recent results are kept only in your own browser, and challenge links carry results in the part of the link after the # sign, which browsers do not send to the website.
