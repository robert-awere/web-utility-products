How many wallpaper rolls do I need?

A drop is one full-height strip of wallpaper. A leftover piece at the end of a roll cannot become a full drop just because its area looks sufficient.

  1. For each wall, divide its width by the roll width and round up. Add these whole-drop counts.
  2. Add wall height and total top-and-bottom trim. For free match, this is the cut length.
  3. For straight match, divide that length by the repeat, round up, then multiply by the repeat.
  4. For straight match only, subtract one repeat from each roll’s length as a conservative starting-alignment reserve.
  5. Divide usable roll length by cut length and round down to full drops per roll.
  6. Divide total drops by drops per roll and round up to whole rolls.

Rolls = round up(total whole drops ÷ full drops per roll)

Our full-repeat roll-start reserve is an explicit conservative modelling choice, not a universal manufacturer formula. It can recommend more rolls than a planned cutting layout.

Worked examples

Four walls of 4, 3, 4 and 3 m, all 2.4 m high, with a 0.52 m × 10.05 m roll and 0.10 m total trim:

  • Free match: 8 + 6 + 8 + 6 = 28 drops. Each is 2.50 m long. Four drops fit in each roll, so plan for 7 rolls.
  • Straight match, 0.64 m repeat: round 2.50 m up to 2.56 m (4 repeats). Reserve 0.64 m per roll, leaving 9.41 m. Three drops fit, so round 28 ÷ 3 up to 10 rolls.

These are sample dimensions, not a standard roll size or a trim recommendation for every wallpaper. Change the visible fields to match your product.

Measure before ordering

Measure each wall’s full width and the greatest floor-to-ceiling height. Use that common height throughout this estimate. For varying heights, recesses, sloped ceilings, stairs or complex corners, ask an installer for a cutting plan. Check matching batch numbers when ordering and agree any extra spare material with your supplier.

Wallpaper calculator questions

Why not subtract doors and windows?

An opening may still need strips above or below it. Subtracting its area does not necessarily save a full-width, full-height drop. Leaving openings included is conservative; an installer may reduce the order with a specific cutting plan.

Can I use a half-drop pattern?

No. Half-drop and other offset matches alternate the pattern position between neighbouring strips. This calculator only models free and straight matching. Follow the product’s instructions or its manufacturer’s calculator.

Does “roll” mean single or double roll?

It means one continuous roll of the width and length you entered. Retailers may price by a single roll but supply double rolls. Match the result to the actual supplied length and pack quantity before ordering.

Do I need extra for mistakes?

The estimate includes the entered trim, whole-drop rounding and the stated straight-match reserve. It adds no separate waste percentage or spare roll. Damage, awkward corners or future repairs may need more. Ask your supplier what reserve makes sense for your job.

What happens when I change units?

The measurements are converted, not reinterpreted. All imperial lengths use decimal feet, not feet-and-inches notation. Full numeric precision is retained to avoid changing a whole-roll boundary.

Are my measurements saved?

No. This page calculates locally in your browser without sending or storing your measurements. Copy is optional; reloading or resetting restores the example.
