How to calculate square ft flooring

Multiply room length by width to find floor area. Add your waste percentage, divide by the coverage per pack, then round up to a whole pack.

Packs = round up[(length × width × (1 + waste %)) ÷ pack coverage]

The calculator converts your entered values when you switch between metric and imperial, rather than reinterpreting the same numbers.

Flooring calculator questions

How much flooring do I need for a 10x20 room?

A 10 × 20 ft room is 200 ft². With 10% waste, plan for 220 ft². Divide 220 by the square feet in each pack and round up.

How much waste should I add?

Ten percent is a common planning allowance for a straightforward room. Complex layouts or diagonal patterns may require more; follow the flooring manufacturer’s guidance.

Does the cost include installation?

No. The flooring calculator cost covers material based on whole packs. Labour, underlay, trims, delivery and tax are excluded.

Is this a flooring calculator app?

Yes—a free browser-based flooring calculator app. It works on mobile and desktop without an account or download.

Are my measurements stored?

No. Calculations run locally in your browser and your room measurements are not sent anywhere.

Flooring calculator m2 and square feet

This floor measurement calculator works for laminate, engineered wood, vinyl plank and other flooring sold by area per pack. Enter the actual coverage shown on the packaging; do not estimate it from plank dimensions when the retailer already supplies a pack figure.

Worked metric example

A 4 m × 3 m floor is 12 m². Adding 10% gives 13.2 m². If each pack covers 2 m², buy 7 packs, providing 14 m². At £25/m², those packs cost about £350.

Flooring calculator cost: what is included?

The cost is based on the whole packs you need: packs × pack coverage × price per m² or ft². It is a material estimate only. Add underlay, adhesive, trims, delivery, tax and installation separately where relevant.

Improve your flooring estimate

  1. Measure at the widest and longest points.
  2. Split L-shaped rooms into rectangles, calculate each section, then add the areas.
  3. Use the exact pack coverage and current unit price from your chosen product.
  4. Check the manufacturer’s waste recommendation for your room shape and laying pattern.
