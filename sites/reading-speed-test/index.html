      How the reading speed test works

      Press Start reading and the clock begins. Read the passage at your normal pace, then press I'm done reading. Five multiple-choice questions follow.

      Your words per minute (wpm) comes from the passage length and your time; your comprehension score, from the questions. A fast time with wrong answers is skimming, not reading.

      What is a good reading speed?

      Average reading speed by age and level

      The best single source is Brysbaert (2019), a review of 190 studies in the Journal of Memory and Language. It puts the adult average at about 238 wpm for non-fiction and 260 wpm for fiction, with reading aloud slower at around 183 wpm.

      Children read more slowly and speed up through school; older adults often slow a little, partly because of vision. Rates also shift with the text and whether it is your first language. We do not publish grade-by-grade targets because figures differ widely between studies and countries.

      How words per minute is calculated

      wpm = words in the passage / (seconds taken / 60)

      Worked example: a 260-word passage read in 1 minute 10 seconds is 260 / (70 / 60) = 222.9, or 223 wpm.

      Effective wpm scales for comprehension: effective wpm = wpm x correct / 5. With 4 of 5 correct, 223 x 0.8 = 178.4, so 178 effective wpm. Against the 238 wpm adult average, 223 wpm shows as about 6 percent slower.

      How to read faster without losing comprehension

      No trick doubles your speed overnight; speed-reading courses mostly trade comprehension for speed. These habits do help:

      • Preview first. Glance at the title and first sentences to see where the text is heading.
      • Stop re-reading. Trust the first pass instead of jumping back.
      • Remove distractions. Every notification costs you a re-read.
      • Grow your vocabulary. Eyes stall on unfamiliar words.
      • Read a little every day, ideally slightly challenging material.

      Assumptions and limitations

      This is a snapshot, not an assessment. It cannot diagnose a reading difficulty or track a child's progress.

      • One short passage is a small sample; repeat on a few for a steadier number.
      • Screen reading is slightly slower than paper for many people.
      • Non-native English readers should expect a lower figure than in their strongest language.
      • Timing runs between your two clicks; a slow reaction adds a second or two.
      • Skimming inflates wpm, which is why comprehension matters more than speed alone.

      FAQ

      What is the average reading speed for adults?

      About 238 words per minute for silent non-fiction reading and about 260 for fiction, according to Brysbaert's 2019 review of 190 studies. Reading aloud averages around 183 wpm.

      What is a good WPM score?

      200 to 300 wpm with four or five questions correct is a normal adult result, and 300 to 400 is above average. Speed only counts as good when comprehension holds up.

      Is 300 words per minute fast?

      Slightly, yes. 300 wpm is about 26 percent faster than the adult non-fiction average of 238 wpm, and sits at the bottom of the Above average band.

      Why does the test ask comprehension questions?

      Because speed alone is meaningless; anyone can scroll a passage in ten seconds. The five questions check that you took in what you read, and effective wpm combines both.

      How accurate is an online reading speed test?

      It is a snapshot, not a formal assessment. Your mood, the screen and how carefully you read all move the number, so try two or three passages and use the typical result.

      Is my result saved or sent anywhere?

      No. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded. A share link contains only the passage id, your wpm and your correct-answer count. No accounts, cookies or analytics.
